# Research: Love Letter (Original) — Complete Official Rules Spec

**Game:** *Love Letter* — original 16-card edition. Design: Seiji Kanai. English publication: Alderac Entertainment Group (AEG), 2012. 2–4 players, ~20 minutes (rounds as short as ~5 min).

**Scope note:** This spec covers ONLY the original AEG 2012 16-card deck. Later editions (2019 Z-Man Second Edition, 2016 Premium, 2024 Z-Man) change the deck; they are cited only where they clarify rulings the original rulebook left ambiguous.

---

## 1. Deck composition (exact)

The original deck is exactly **16 cards**, plus 4 reference cards (memory aids, not played) and red tokens of affection. Each card has a value in the upper-left corner — the higher the number, the closer that person is to the Princess — and a text box describing its effect when discarded.

| Value | Card | Count | Effect (official rulebook text) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Guard | 5 | Choose a player and name a card (other than Guard). If that player has that card, that player is knocked out of the round. |
| 2 | Priest | 2 | You can look at one other player's hand. Do not reveal the hand to all players. |
| 3 | Baron | 2 | Choose one other player still in the round. You and that player secretly compare your hands. The player with the lower rank is knocked out. In case of a tie, nothing happens. |
| 4 | Handmaid | 2 | You are immune to the effects of other players' cards until the start of your next turn. |
| 5 | Prince | 2 | Choose one player still in the round (including yourself). That player discards his or her hand (do not apply its effect) and draws a new card. |
| 6 | King | 1 | Trade the card in your hand with the card held by another player of your choice. |
| 7 | Countess | 1 | No effect when discarded. If you ever have the Countess and either the King or Prince in your hand, you must discard the Countess. |
| 8 | Princess | 1 | If you discard the Princess — no matter how or why — you are knocked out of the round. |

Sum: 5+2+2+2+2+1+1+1 = **16 cards**.

Sources: [AEG 2012 rulebook (verbatim text)](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/83/b2/32-love-letter-rulebook.pdf) (components p.7; card profiles pp.15–23); [Wikipedia card table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Letter_(card_game)) (which cites the archived official AEG rules PDF: [Love_Letter_Rules_Final.pdf, 2012](https://web.archive.org/web/20160518034814/http://www.alderac.com/tempest/files/2012/09/Love_Letter_Rules_Final.pdf)); [UltraBoardGames — The People (official card text)](https://www.ultraboardgames.com/love-letter/the-people.php); [What's Eric Playing review (deck counts)](https://whatsericplaying.com/2016/02/14/love-letter/).

## 2. Setup per player count (2, 3, 4)

Official setup (same for all counts except the 2-player extra removal):

1. Shuffle the 16 cards into a face-down draw deck.
2. **Remove the top card of the deck from the game without looking at it** (face-down, out of play for the round).
3. **2-player only:** take **three more cards** from the top of the deck and place them to the side, **face up**. They will not be used during this round. (3–4 players: no additional removal.)
4. Deal **one card to each player**, face down. This is the player's hand, kept secret.
5. First player: whoever was most recently on a date (if tied, the younger player wins the tie). In later rounds, the winner of the previous round goes first.

Cards in the draw deck at start of round:
- **2 players:** 16 − 1 face-down removed − 3 face-up removed − 2 dealt = **10**.
- **3 players:** 16 − 1 removed − 3 dealt = **12**.
- **4 players:** 16 − 1 removed − 4 dealt = **11**.

Sources: [AEG 2012 rulebook (setup p.9)](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/83/b2/32-love-letter-rulebook.pdf); [UltraBoardGames — Setup](https://www.ultraboardgames.com/love-letter/game-rules.php); [BGG thread on the 2-player face-up removal](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/983770/two-player-games-removing-three-cards-and-setting); [Dicebreaker — Setup](https://www.dicebreaker.com/games/love-letter/how-to/how-to-play-love-letter). Note the same 2-player rule persists in current editions: [Z-Man 2024 rulebook](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-zman/uploads/2024/09/LL_Rulebook_with_Bag-1.pdf).

## 3. Full turn structure

- On your turn, **draw the top card** of the deck and add it to your hand (you now hold 2 cards).
- **Choose one of the two cards** in your hand and **discard it face up in front of you**.
- **Apply any effect on the card you discarded. You must apply its effect, even if it is bad for you** (e.g., a Baron that knocks you out, a Prince you must play on yourself).
- All discarded cards remain face up in front of the player who discarded them, **overlapped in order**, so the table can track what has been played.
- When the effect resolves, the turn passes to the player on the left.
- **Out of the round:** a player knocked out discards the card in hand **face up without applying its effect**, and takes no more turns until the next round.
- **Honesty note (rulebook):** players could cheat when chosen with the Guard or fail to discard the Countess with King/Prince in hand; the rulebook simply advises not playing with knaves.

Sources: [AEG 2012 rulebook (pp.10–12)](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/83/b2/32-love-letter-rulebook.pdf); [UltraBoardGames — Game Play](https://www.ultraboardgames.com/love-letter/game-rules.php); mandatory-effect confirmation: [StackExchange #24536](https://boardgames.stackexchange.com/questions/24536/in-love-letter-can-you-discard-a-card-instead-of-playing-it).

## 4. The eight card effects — exact resolution

### 1 — Guard (×5)
"When you discard the Guard, choose a player and name a card (other than Guard). If that player has that card, that player is knocked out of the round."
- The named card must be one of the other 7 card types; **Guard may never be named**.
- The guess is correct only if the target's *hand card* matches the named type exactly. A wrong guess has no effect and reveals nothing.
- If all other players still in the round are protected by the Handmaid, this card does nothing.
- When the target is eliminated, they reveal/discard their hand face up (out-of-round rule) — which confirms or refutes the guess publicly.

### 2 — Priest (×2)
"When you discard the Priest, you can look at one other player's hand. Do not reveal the hand to all players." — The information is private to the Priest player only. If all other players are Handmaid-protected, the card does nothing.

### 3 — Baron (×2)
"When discarded, choose one other player still in the round. You and that player secretly compare your hands. The player with the lower rank is knocked out of the round. In case of a tie, nothing happens."
- Comparison is between the **card remaining in your hand** and the **target's hand card** — the discarded Baron itself is not compared.
- Equal values (e.g., both hold a Prince): nobody is eliminated.
- If all other players are Handmaid-protected, the card does nothing.

### 4 — Handmaid (×2)
"When you discard the Handmaid, you are immune to the effects of other players' cards until the start of your next turn."
- Scope: only **other players'** card effects are blocked — Guard, Priest, Baron, Prince, and King cannot choose/target you. Your own cards (e.g., a Prince you play on yourself) still work on you.
- The immunity ends at **the start of your next turn** (i.e., it spans the other players' turns back around to you; it does not last the whole round, and it does not carry past round end).
- It does **not** block the Countess's forced-discard condition, because that is a property of your own hand, not another player's card effect.
- General 2+ player consequence (rulebook, Handmaid section): "If all players other than the player whose turn it is are protected by [the Handmaid], the player must choose him- or herself if possible." Concretely: Guard/Priest/Baron/King have no legal target and do nothing; Prince must target yourself.

### 5 — Prince (×2)
"When you discard Prince Arnaud, choose one player still in the round (including yourself). That player discards his or her hand (do not apply its effect) and draws a new card. If the deck is empty, that player draws the card that was removed at the start of the round."
- The discarded card's **effect is NOT resolved** (a discarded Baron/Guard/etc. does nothing).
- Exception: the **Princess** rule is not a card effect — "If you discard the Princess, no matter how or why, you are knocked out of the round" — so **Prince-ing a player holding the Princess eliminates that player** (and they do not draw a replacement, because a knocked-out player takes no further action; see §6 edge cases).
- **Prince-ing yourself is legal and mandatory when everyone else is Handmaid-protected.** If your only choice is yourself and you hold the Princess, you are forced to eliminate yourself.
- If the deck is empty when the target must draw, they draw the single face-down card removed at setup (see §7 edge cases).
- Target must be a player **still in the round**.

### 6 — King (×1)
"When you discard King Arnaud IV, trade the card in your hand with the card held by another player of your choice. You cannot trade with a player who is out of the round, nor with someone protected by the Handmaid. If all other players still in the round are protected by the Handmaid, this card does nothing."
- Trading is **not** "discarding": a Princess swapped away does not eliminate you — she simply changes hands (see §7). (A player who receives the King while holding the Countess must then immediately discard the Countess — §7.)
- You may not trade with yourself (target must be *another* player).

### 7 — Countess (×1)
"Unlike other cards, which take effect when discarded, the text on the Countess applies while she is in your hand. In fact, she has no effect when you discard her. **If you ever have the Countess and either the King or Prince in your hand, you must discard the Countess.** You do not have to reveal the other card in your hand. Of course, you can also discard the Countess even if you do not have a royal family member in your hand."
- The constraint is **mandatory and immediate** whenever the condition becomes true — when you draw a King/Prince while holding the Countess, or when a King is traded into your hand while you hold the Countess. You may **not** discard the King/Prince instead.
- The forced discard **has no effect** and you do **not** reveal your other card.
- **Voluntary** Countess discard is always allowed (it does nothing).
- The constraint applies **only** to holding King or Prince — NOT to holding the Princess (a common misconception). Holding Countess + Princess forces nothing; you merely choose (and would never discard the Princess, since that eliminates you).

### 8 — Princess (×1)
"If you discard the Princess—no matter how or why—she has tossed your letter into the fire. You are knocked out of the round."
- Applies to: choosing to play the Princess, being forced to discard her by the Prince, or any other discard. It does **not** apply to *trading* her via the King (a trade is not a discard), nor to holding her.

Sources for §4: [AEG 2012 rulebook, card profiles pp.15–23 (verbatim)](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/83/b2/32-love-letter-rulebook.pdf); [UltraBoardGames — The People](https://www.ultraboardgames.com/love-letter/the-people.php); [Wikipedia card table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Letter_(card_game)); Handmaid-forced-self-target ruling: [StackExchange #27839 (quotes rulebook pp.19–20)](https://boardgames.stackexchange.com/questions/27839/love-letter-handmaiden-card-play) and the [2019 Z-Man rulebook FAQ](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/c0/d4/2b-love-letter-2019-rulebook.pdf) ("If all other players are protected by a Handmaid: Guard/Priest/Baron/King play with no effect; Prince must choose yourself").

## 5. Two-player special rules

- **Setup:** besides the standard 1 face-down removed card, remove **3 more cards face up** (visible to both players) and set them aside unused for the round. Total removed: 4 (1 hidden + 3 public). Deck: 10 cards. [AEG 2012 rulebook p.9](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/83/b2/32-love-letter-rulebook.pdf); [BGG thread #983770](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/983770/two-player-games-removing-three-cards-and-setting).
- **Guard/Priest/Baron/King with exactly one opponent:** the only legal target is your opponent. If the opponent is Handmaid-protected, the card **does nothing** — there is no alternative target and no self-target fallback for these four cards. [AEG 2012 rulebook (per-card "If all other players… protected" clauses)](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/83/b2/32-love-letter-rulebook.pdf).
- **Prince:** if your opponent is Handmaid-protected, you **must choose yourself** (the only remaining legal target) — even if that eliminates you (e.g., you hold the Princess). [AEG 2012 rulebook p.19 and Handmaid note p.20](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/83/b2/32-love-letter-rulebook.pdf).
- **Baron ties:** comparing equal values eliminates nobody; play continues.
- **First player:** no special 2-player rule — same "most recently on a date" rule; winner of the previous round starts the next.
- No other 2-player-specific rules exist in the original rulebook.

## 6. End-of-round scoring and win condition

A round ends when **either**:
1. **The deck is empty at the end of a turn** — all players still in the round reveal their hands; the player holding the **highest-value card wins the round**. "In case of a tie, the player who discarded the highest total value of cards wins" (sum the values of each tied player's face-up discard pile).
2. **All players but one are out of the round** — the last remaining player immediately wins the round (no hand comparison).

The round winner receives **one token of affection**. Then shuffle all 16 cards together and set up a new round (all setup rules, including removals, apply again); the previous round's winner goes first.

**Game win:** a player wins the game upon accumulating the target number of tokens for their player count (below).

**Full-tie edge case:** the original English rulebook does not say what happens if two players tie on hand value AND on total discarded value (e.g., both held Barons and discarded identical totals). The Nordic editions and AEG's later Premium edition (2016) resolved it: "If there is still a tie, then all tied players are considered to have won the round" (each tied winner receives a token). The 2019 Z-Man edition likewise gives each tied player a favor token. The original 2012 text is silent — tables typically replay the round or award each tied player a token (the ruling AEG later adopted).

Sources: [AEG 2012 rulebook pp.13–14](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/83/b2/32-love-letter-rulebook.pdf); [UltraBoardGames — End of a Round](https://www.ultraboardgames.com/love-letter/game-rules.php); full-tie discussion incl. Nordic rulebook: [StackExchange #23924](https://boardgames.stackexchange.com/questions/23924/how-can-we-determine-the-winner-in-love-letter-if-the-last-two-players-tie-and-h); Premium tie rule: [AEG Premium rulebook 2016](https://alderac.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/Love-Letter-Premium_Rulebook.pdf); current edition: [Z-Man 2024 rulebook](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-zman/uploads/2024/09/LL_Rulebook_with_Bag-1.pdf).

## 8. Edge cases

1. **Deck runs empty mid-effect (Prince):** "If the deck is empty, that player draws the card that was removed at the start of the round" — the single face-down card set aside at setup is pulled into the target's hand. The round still ends at the end of the current turn ("a round ends if the deck is empty at the end of a turn" — the last card drawn is still played). If the removed card is the Princess, the drawn player now holds the 8 and wins the round. [AEG 2012 rulebook p.19](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/83/b2/32-love-letter-rulebook.pdf); also confirmed in review coverage: [What's Eric Playing](https://whatsericplaying.com/2016/02/14/love-letter/).
   - **2-player nuance:** four cards were removed (1 face-down + 3 face-up). The face-up three are explicitly "not used during this round"; the Prince-draw clause refers to the face-down removed card, so the face-up cards are never drawn. The face-down card is the only candidate. (Slight textual ambiguity — "the card" singular — resolved by the "will not be used" clause; worth knowing your table's ruling.)
2. **Prince on a player holding the Princess:** the target discards the Princess and is **immediately out of the round** — the Princess's elimination clause overrides the Prince's "draw a new card" instruction (a knocked-out player "takes no more turns" and does not draw). This applies whether the Prince is played by an opponent or by the player on themselves. [AEG 2012 rulebook pp.15–16, 19](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/83/b2/32-love-letter-rulebook.pdf); [Z-Man edition rule text](https://www.64ouncegames.com/pages/love-letter-zman) ("If you either play or discard the Princess for any reason, you are immediately out of the round").
3. **Handmaid protection scope:** blocks **all card effects from other players** — you cannot be *chosen* by another player's Guard, Priest, Baron, Prince, or King — until the **start of your next turn**. It does NOT block: your own card effects (self-Prince), the Countess forced discard (your own hand condition), or round-end scoring. It is not permanent and does not extend past the start of your next turn. [AEG 2012 rulebook p.20](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/83/b2/32-love-letter-rulebook.pdf); [StackExchange #27839](https://boardgames.stackexchange.com/questions/27839/love-letter-handmaiden-card-play); [2019 Z-Man FAQ](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/c0/d4/2b-love-letter-2019-rulebook.pdf).
4. **King trading with a protected (or eliminated) player:** explicitly illegal — "You cannot trade with a player who is out of the round, nor with someone protected by the Handmaid." If every other remaining player is protected, the King does nothing. [AEG 2012 rulebook p.18](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/83/b2/32-love-letter-rulebook.pdf).
5. **King trading the Princess:** legal — a trade is not a discard, so the Princess leaves your hand without eliminating you (the recipient now holds the 8; if the round ends they win). [BGG thread #1741015](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/1741015/question-about-princess-and-king).
6. **Countess timing:** the forced discard triggers whenever the condition is true — including immediately after a King is traded into your hand (or after you draw a King/Prince). The King-trade scenario is the classic rules-lawyer corner; the "if you ever have" wording supports immediate resolution (as implemented by Board Game Arena and the later rulebooks). [AEG 2012 rulebook p.17](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/83/b2/32-love-letter-rulebook.pdf); [BGA rules help](https://en.doc.boardgamearena.com/Gamehelploveletter); [BGG thread #2570129](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2570129/urgent-drawing-the-countess-and-the-princes-means).
7. **Effects are mandatory, even self-destructive:** "You must apply its effect, even if it is bad for you" — e.g., forced self-Prince when everyone else is Handmaid-protected, or a Baron compare you lose. [AEG 2012 rulebook p.11](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/83/b2/32-love-letter-rulebook.pdf); [StackExchange #24536](https://boardgames.stackexchange.com/questions/24536/in-love-letter-can-you-discard-a-card-instead-of-playing-it).
8. **Eliminated player's hand is revealed:** knocked-out players discard their hand face up without resolving its effect — this publicly confirms/refutes Guard guesses and Baron comparisons. [AEG 2012 rulebook p.12](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/83/b2/32-love-letter-rulebook.pdf).
9. **Baron ties:** equal hand values eliminate nobody — both stay in the round. [AEG 2012 rulebook p.21](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/83/b2/32-love-letter-rulebook.pdf).
10. **Guard naming limits:** you may name any card type except Guard. Naming a type already fully revealed is legal but wasted; naming the *value* of a card held by a Handmaid-protected player is pointless because that player cannot be chosen at all.

## 9. Commonly misimplemented or ambiguous details worth flagging

1. **Countess is mandatory, not optional, and only for King/Prince.** Many players treat it as a choice or apply it to the Princess. It triggers only with King or Prince in hand, is immediate, and the Countess itself does nothing when discarded. (Mis-ruled frequently; the rulebook even has an "honesty" sidebar about it.)
2. **Countess vs. Princess hand.** Holding Countess + Princess forces nothing; you discard the Countess by *choice* because discarding the Princess eliminates you. Also: the Countess does NOT force a discard when you hold the Princess.
3. **Prince discard is "without effect."** A Prince'd card's effect does not fire — except the Princess elimination, which is not an effect but a discard clause. People often wrongly resolve the discarded card's effect.
4. **Prince'd Princess = eliminated AND no replacement draw.** The "draw a new card" instruction is skipped for a knocked-out player.
5. **Handmaid timing/scope.** Immunity lasts only until the start of your next turn (not "the rest of the round"); it blocks only other players' card effects; it does not stop your own Countess/Prince; you cannot even be *chosen* as a target.
6. **Forced self-targeting.** When all other players are Handmaid-protected: Guard/Priest/Baron/King fizzle ("do nothing"), Prince must target yourself. Many tables forget the Prince self-target rule.
7. **King is not a discard.** Trading a Princess away is legal and does not eliminate you; receiving a King while holding the Countess forces the immediate Countess discard.
8. **Guard cannot name Guard** — and in the original text says "choose a player" (not "another player") while Priest/Baron/King explicitly say "other/another." Self-targeting with Guard is degenerate (never beneficial; eliminates you only if the guess is right about your own card), and every later edition says "another player" — treat self-targeting as disallowed.
9. **Round-end tie-break is by discard totals, and a full tie is undefined in the 2012 text.** Later editions award the token to all tied players; the original rulebook is silent.
10. **Deck-empty Prince draw uses the hidden removed card** — not the top of the discard pile, not the face-up 2-player cards.
11. **2-player face-up removal.** Three cards are removed face up in 2-player games (players can see them and should factor them into Guard guesses); they are never used that round, including by the Prince's empty-deck draw.
12. **Discard order visibility.** Players are encouraged to overlap discards in order; the out-of-round hand reveal is public. Discarding face up is mandatory (only the Priest's look, Baron's compare, and King's trade are private exchanges).

## Gaps

1. **Full-tie round resolution** (same hand value AND same discarded total) is genuinely undefined in the original 2012 English rulebook; the later-edition ruling (all tied players win a token) is cited as the resolution AEG adopted, but no original-edition official text exists.
2. **Countess timing after a King trade** is the mainstream reading ("if you ever have…") and is how BGA implements it, but the original text does not use the word "immediately"; a minority table interpretation defers the discard to the player's own turn. Flagged in §8.6 rather than asserted as unambiguous.
3. **Guard self-targeting** is textually possible in the original ("choose a player") but degenerate and disallowed in every later edition; convention treats it as targeting another player only.
4. **Prince's empty-deck draw in 2-player** — "the card that was removed at the start of the round" is read as the single face-down card (the face-up three are explicitly unused), but the clause is singular while four cards were removed; no official FAQ addresses the 2-player case explicitly.
5. **Suggested next step:** if implementation-grade certainty is needed for the flagged items (2, 4), consult the official AEG FAQ/forum (alderac.com/forum, now defunct) via the Wayback Machine, or Z-Man's current FAQ, which inherits these rulings.
